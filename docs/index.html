Why counts disagree, and how this one works

Published counts mix three different numbers: people with permanent implants, people with temporary implants placed during surgery, and trial enrollment targets. They also include or leave out China, and rarely subtract devices that were removed. The census keeps these apart and counts only permanent implants with a source.

Each program's floor is the larger of its latest sourced count and the actual enrollment of its permanent-implant trials that are still following participants or ended in the last five years. Older trials are listed but not counted, because their participants may have had devices removed. People are counted unless a source reports a removal or death.

Each fact has a tier: A registry or peer-reviewed paper, B company statement, C credible press report. Aggregator figures (D) are shown below for context and never counted. Full rules: METHODOLOGY.md.

Claims waiting for a source

Have one? Open an issue with the link and it gets credited.

  • Neuralink: Neuralink's implant count after January 2026
    Company updates and Reuters coverage since January 2026. Counts of 26 to 27 circulate on fan blogs only.
  • BrainGate: Cumulative people implanted in the BrainGate studies
    BrainGate publications. The registry cannot help yet: BrainGate2 (NCT00912041) is still recruiting and lists only estimated enrollment.
  • Academic and other: Implants at Pittsburgh, Caltech, UCSF, Johns Hopkins, UMC Utrecht and other academic programs
    Peer-reviewed papers and trial records; watch for people counted twice across programs.
  • Precision Neuroscience: Precision's count of temporary implant procedures (reported as more than 100)
    Check the exact wording in Precision's Sept 24, 2026 Series D release.
  • Axoft: Whether Axoft's 11 first-in-human implants were temporary or permanent
    ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06673264 (5 people, Panama) inserted the probe during already scheduled brain surgery, so those were temporary. The other 6 need a source.
  • StairMed: StairMed's first-in-human implants
    Company releases and Chinese press.
  • Onward ARC-BCI: People implanted with Onward's ARC-BCI system
    Onward, EPFL and Clinatec releases and papers. EPFL's four ARC-BSI and STIMO-BSI registry records list only estimated enrollment.
  • CorTec Brain Interchange: People implanted with CorTec's Brain Interchange for communication or movement
    Company releases and papers. Its registry records so far (Mayo Clinic, NCT05439655 and NCT06898138) use it for epilepsy, which is out of scope.
  • Academic and other: Reported removals (explants) or deaths among BCI recipients
    Publications, company statements and FDA MAUDE adverse-event reports.
